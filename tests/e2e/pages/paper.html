Gut microbiome and immunotherapy

Introduction

Response to checkpoint blockade varies widely [1, 2]. Several cohorts link it to the gut microbiome (Smith et al., 2020; Lee & Kim, 2019). The idea is simple: bacteria shape the immune system, and the immune system decides whether a tumour shrinks.

Fecal transplants restored response in mice. Doses reached ten million cells per animal. Responders carried more short-chain fatty acid producers, while non-responders carried more inflammatory taxa.

Data are at https://example.org/data/set1 and doi:10.1038/s41586-020-1234-5. The cohorts were collected in four countries over six years, with stool samples taken before the first dose.

Methods

Samples were sequenced with shotgun metagenomics. Reads were trimmed, host reads were removed, and species abundance was estimated with a reference database. Diversity was compared between responders and non-responders.

Models were trained on abundance profiles to predict response. Each model was tested on a cohort it had not seen, so that a good score could not come from one hospital's habits.

Statistical tests used a false discovery rate of ten percent. Effect sizes are reported with confidence intervals, because a small p value alone says little about how large a difference is.

Results

Diversity was higher in responders in three of four cohorts. The fourth cohort was small and its interval crossed zero, so it neither supports nor contradicts the pattern.

Prediction across cohorts was modest. The best model reached an area under the curve of about seven tenths, which is useful for research but far from a clinical test.

Several species appeared in every cohort's top list. Their presence alone did not predict response well, which suggests that the community as a whole matters more than any single species.

Discussion

The microbiome is one factor among many. Tumour mutation burden, prior treatment and antibiotics all change the picture, and a fair model has to include them.

Future work should follow patients over time, because a single stool sample is a snapshot of a community that changes with diet, drugs and illness.

Как показали Иванов и др. (2021), эффект воспроизводится в независимых когортах [3, с. 25]. Это важно для переноса результатов в клинику.

In summary, the gut microbiome is linked to the response to immunotherapy, but the link is not yet strong enough to guide treatment on its own.
